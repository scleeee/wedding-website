**\# THE ESSENTIALS: A SMOOTH LANDING**

***\#\# How do I communicate with locals?***  
You can get by just fine with English\! English is widely spoken throughout the Philippines, especially in Manila, so don't be shy about asking for directions, ordering food, or chatting with locals. Thanks, US colonialism lol.

That said, knowing a few Filipino words will definitely earn you some smiles and make your trip a little more fun. Here are a few you'll probably hear often:

* SALAMAT — Thank you  
* MAHAL — Expensive OR "love/dear," depending on the context  
* MAGKANO? — How much?  
* SAAN? — Where?  
* SARAP — Delicious / yummy  
* PO / OPO — Polite/respectful words, especially when speaking to elders  
* TARA\! — Let's go\!  
* SANDALI — Wait a moment  
* ATE — Literally "older sister," but commonly used to politely address a woman who is a little older than you  
* KUYA — Literally "older brother," but commonly used to politely address a man who is a little older than you  
* CR — Short for "Comfort Room," aka the bathroom/restroom  
* KAIN TAYO\! — Let's eat\!  
* INGAT\! — Take care / stay safe  
* OO — Yes  
* HINDI — No

Filipino conversations often mix English and Filipino, a style commonly called "Taglish." So don't be surprised if someone starts a sentence in English and finishes it in Filipino. You'll probably understand more than you think\!

***\#\# How do I get Philippine Pesos (money)?***  
Most restaurants, malls, hotels, and other major establishments accept major credit cards, especially Visa and Mastercard. American Express is less commonly accepted, so don't rely on it as your only card.

For smaller shops, local vendors, taxis, and other cash-only situations, it's still a good idea to have some Philippine pesos (₱) on hand. You can withdraw ₱ from ATMs or exchange foreign currency at reputable money changers. The best places to exchange foreign cash are established money changers in major malls or busy commercial areas, rather than the airport.  We recommend getting just enough cash for your first Grab ride and immediate expenses, then withdrawing or exchanging more once you're settled in Manila.

TIP: When using an ATM, always choose to be charged in PHP rather than your home currency.

***\#\# What’s the weather like in January?***  
January is a pretty pleasant time to visit Metro Manila. It's one of the cooler and drier months, although "cool" by Manila standards still means you won't be reaching for a winter coat. Expect warm days, comfortable evenings, and much less rain than during the summer months.

Temperature in daytime will range from \~25–30°C (77–86°F) and evenings will range from \~21–25°C (70–77°F).

We recommend packing lightweight, breathable fabrics for your trip. Examples include linen, cotton, rayon, and other lightweight materials that let you stay cool and comfortable.

TIP: Bring a light layer for heavily air-conditioned restaurants, malls, and hotels. Manila's indoor air-conditioning can sometimes feel like a completely different climate.

**\# GETTING AROUND MANILA**  
Manila may feel a little overwhelming at first, but getting around becomes much easier once you know your options. For first-time visitors, we recommend prioritizing convenience and comfort over saving a few pesos. Here’s our quick guide to getting around Manila:

***\#\# GRAB: YOUR BEST FRIEND 🚗***

Grab works similarly to Uber. Grab is especially useful for:  
• Airport transfers  
• Getting around at night  
• Traveling with luggage  
• Going somewhere unfamiliar  
• Avoiding the hassle of negotiating fares

GOOD TO KNOW: You do NOT need a Philippine phone number to use Grab. You can download and set up the app before your trip using your existing foreign phone number or email, as long as you can receive the verification code.

TIP: Download and set up Grab before you leave home so you're ready to go as soon as you land\!

***\#\# ANGKAS: BEAT THE TRAFFIC 🏍️***

Angkas is a motorcycle ride-hailing service and one of the quickest ways to get around Manila, especially when traffic is being... very Manila.

You book a ride through the Angkas app, and a registered motorcycle rider picks you up and takes you to your destination. Helmets are provided.

Angkas is great for shorter trips or busy roads where cars can get stuck in traffic. Just keep in mind that you'll be riding on a motorcycle, so travel light and secure anything that might decide to fly away.

Not comfortable on a motorcycle? No problem. Grab is our go-to alternative\!

***\#\# TAXIS 🚕***

Metered taxis are widely available throughout Manila and can be a good backup if Grab cars are unavailable or prices are unusually high.

If you take a taxi, make sure the driver turns on the meter before you start your trip. While most drivers are perfectly fine, you may occasionally encounter someone who tries to offer a fixed fare instead.

It's also a good idea to carry some cash, as not every taxi accepts credit cards.

***\#\# TRAINS 🚆***

Manila has three main urban rail lines: MRT-3, LRT-1, and LRT-2. Trains can be a fast and affordable way to travel, especially during rush hour when traffic is at its worst.

Google Maps is helpful for figuring out which line and station to use. If your route lines up with a station, trains can save you a lot of time.

The catch? They can get VERY crowded during peak hours, and navigating the stations can take a little getting used to. Some stations also have limited elevators and escalators.

For first-time visitors, we'd recommend using the trains when you're feeling adventurous or when they make significantly more sense for your route. Otherwise, Grab is usually easier.

***\#\# WALKING 🚶***

Walking isn't always the most practical way to get around Manila. The heat, traffic, and occasionally uneven sidewalks can make a short distance feel much longer than it looks on a map.

That said, some areas are very walkable\!

BGC is one of the most pedestrian-friendly areas in Metro Manila, with wide sidewalks, clear signage, and plenty of restaurants and shops within walking distance.

Parts of Makati are also great for walking, especially around Greenbelt, Glorietta, and the surrounding business district.

***And one final piece of Manila wisdom:***

***If Google Maps says your destination is 20 minutes away, give yourself extra time because it will realistically take an hour.***

***\#\# Where should I go as a first-time visitor in Manila?***  
Shawn & I love exploring Metro Manila, and there’s a lot more to see than just the usual tourist spots. Whether you want history, art, good food, nightlife, or a break from the city, there’s something for you.

One important thing to know: Metro Manila traffic is very real. 😅 Plan your day around geography and give yourself plenty of travel time between destinations. You don't need to see everything\! Manila is a huge, sprawling metro, and trying to squeeze too many neighborhoods into one day will have you spending more time in traffic than actually exploring. Pick one or two areas, take your time, eat something delicious, and enjoy the city at your own pace.

Most tours and experiences can be booked through Klook, so it's a good place to start if you'd like to explore with a guide.

\#\#\# IF YOU WANT TO STEP INTO PHILIPPINE HISTORY: INTRAMUROS 🏛️

Intramuros, meaning "within the walls," is Manila's historic walled city and one of the best places to learn about the Philippines' Spanish colonial history.

Walk along its old stone streets, visit historic churches and museums, and explore the centuries-old fortifications. We recommend joining a guided walking or biking tour to get more context about the history behind what you're seeing.

\#\#\# IF YOU WANT TO EXPERIENCE MODERN MANILA: BGC 🌆

Bonifacio Global City (BGC) is one of the most modern and walkable parts of Metro Manila. Think wide sidewalks, public art, trendy restaurants, cafés, shopping, and plenty of places to grab a drink.

It's a great option if you want an easy day of exploring without having to worry too much about navigating the city. Start around Bonifacio High Street and wander from there.

\#\#\# IF YOU WANT FOOD, HISTORY & A LITTLE CHAOS: BINONDO 🥟🏮

Welcome to Binondo, Manila's Chinatown and the oldest Chinatown in the world, established in 1594\. Binondo is a lively mix of Filipino and Chinese culture, packed with restaurants, bakeries, tea shops, markets, jewelry stores, and historic churches.

Come for the food and stay for the wandering. Try everything from dumplings and noodles to hopia, tikoy, milk tea, and other Filipino-Chinese treats. A food crawl is one of the best ways to experience the neighborhood\!

We recommend going with an appetite and comfortable shoes. Binondo can be crowded, bustling, and a little chaotic, but that's part of the fun. 😆

TIP: Binondo is best explored on foot, so give yourself a few hours rather than trying to squeeze it into a quick stop.

***\#\# What is Philippine Mall Culture?***  
One thing you should know about Manila: we take our malls very seriously.

In the Philippines, the mall is more than a place to shop. It's a meeting place, dining destination, entertainment hub, refuge from the heat, and sometimes an entire day's itinerary. "Let's go to the mall" can mean anything from grabbing coffee to having dinner, watching a movie, buying groceries, and somehow spending five hours there.

If you're staying in Makati, BGC, or Ortigas, you're in luck. Some of Manila's best malls are right in your backyard.

\#\#\# MALLS IN MAKATI

🌿 Rockwell  
Rockwell is polished but relaxed, with great restaurants, cafés, shops, and plenty of places to wander. It's a nice escape from the busier parts of Manila.

🌳 Greenbelt  
Located in the heart of Makati, Greenbelt is a beautiful open-air mall complex surrounded by greenery, restaurants, cafés, and shops. It's especially nice in the evening when the weather cools down. Greenbelt 3 and 5 are great places to look for dinner and drinks.

\#\#\# MALLS IN BGC

🌴 Bonifacio High Street  
Technically more of a shopping and dining district than a traditional enclosed mall, High Street is one of our favorite places to walk around. It's pedestrian-friendly, lined with restaurants and shops, and especially pleasant in the evening.

🛍️ SM Aura  
Convenient, modern, and connected to Bonifacio High Street. Great for shopping, restaurants, groceries, and escaping the Manila heat. Kultura is also here, so it's a convenient place to pick up a Barong Tagalog if you haven't gotten one yet.

🌃 Mitsukoshi BGC  
If you're craving Japanese food, snacks, or a little taste of Tokyo in Manila, head here. It's a newer Japanese-inspired shopping and dining destination in BGC and a fun place to explore.

☕ One Bonifacio High Street  
A convenient stop if you're already around High Street, with restaurants, cafés, and shops all within easy walking distance.

\#\#\# MALLS IN ORTIGAS

**🛍️** Greenhills Shopping Center  
 If you want to experience Manila shopping at its most lively, Greenhills is the place. It's a sprawling shopping complex packed with stalls and stores selling everything from clothes, accessories, electronics, home goods, Filipino souvenirs, and specialty items.

Greenhills is less polished and curated than places like Rockwell or Greenbelt, which is exactly the point. Come ready to wander, browse, compare prices, and bargain. It's a fun place to pick up pasalubong (souvenirs/gifts to bring home), gifts, and random things you didn't know you needed.

✨ Shangri-La Plaza  
A longtime favorite of ours for shopping and dining. This is basically Lizzy’s second home. It's connected to the EDSA Shangri-La area and conveniently accessible via the MRT, making it an easy stop if you're exploring Manila by train.

🍽️ The Podium  
Sleek, modern, and one of our go-tos for food. The Podium has a great selection of restaurants and cafés and is particularly nice if you're looking for a more polished dining experience.

🏙️ SM Megamall  
Come here for the full Filipino mall experience. It's enormous. There are seemingly endless shops, restaurants, entertainment options, and everything else you could possibly need. You may enter looking for socks and leave three hours later having had lunch, dinner, and 5 shopping bags. This is normal.

***\#\# What are your food recommendations?***

\#\#\# SHAWN & LIZZY'S TOP 10 FAVORITE RESTAURANTS IN METRO MANILA 🍽️

We love food, and these are some of the places we find ourselves coming back to. We've loosely ranked them from more affordable to more of a splurge, but honestly, you can't go too wrong with any of these.

1. JT'S MANUKAN 🇵🇭  
   Budget-friendly Filipino comfort food, specializing in Bacolod-style chicken inasal. Come hungry and order the chicken, garlic rice, and a few sides to share. It's casual, unfussy, and very Filipino.  
2. COCO ICHIBANYA 🇯🇵  
   Our go-to for Japanese curry. The fun part is customizing your curry with your choice of protein, toppings, rice, and spice level. Perfect for an easy, casual meal.  
3. YABU 🇯🇵  
   If you like tonkatsu, go here. Crispy, juicy Japanese-style pork or chicken cutlets served with cabbage, rice, miso soup, and their signature sesame dipping sauce.  
4. MASAN 🇰🇷  
   A Korean favorite of ours for hearty, comforting food. Great for sharing, especially if you're in the mood for Korean barbecue, hot soups, and all the banchan that come with it.  
5. MANAM 🇵🇭  
   One of our favorite places to introduce people to Filipino food. Manam takes classic Filipino dishes and gives them a modern twist. Order several dishes and share everything\! We love ordering the sisig and the watermelon sinigang.  
6. CIBO 🇮🇹  
   Modern Italian food with a Filipino favorite status. CIBO has been around for decades and serves everything from pastas and pizzas to salads and desserts. It's an easy choice when you're craving Italian food in Manila.  
7. NIHONBASHITEI 🇯🇵  
   A longtime Makati favorite for Japanese food. Think sushi, sashimi, tempura, teppanyaki, and generous lunch sets. It's casual, authentic-feeling, and particularly good value for the quality.  
8. HALF SAINTS 🍽️  
   A cozy neighborhood restaurant serving creative, contemporary food. This is one of those places where you order a bunch of things for the table and keep discovering new favorites. Don't skip dessert\!  
9. GLORIA MARIS 🥢  
   A Manila institution for Chinese food, especially seafood and dim sum. This is a great place for a big group meal where everyone can order several dishes for the table. Come hungry and embrace the Filipino-Chinese tradition of sharing everything.  
10. HAPAG ✨  
    For the big splurge. Hapag serves contemporary Filipino cuisine through a tasting-menu experience, showcasing Filipino ingredients and flavors in a more elevated, fine-dining format. If you want to make one dinner in Manila feel like an occasion, this is the one.
