For our out-of-town guests

Manila, with a little help from us

Manila is warm, lively, delicious, and occasionally very slow-moving. Here’s the short version of how to arrive smoothly, dress the part, and find a few things worth lingering for.

The essentials

A smooth landing

Save these before your flight, then let the city do the rest.

01

Arrive with data

An eSIM is the easiest route if your phone supports it. Globe and Smart both offer tourist options; otherwise, pick up a prepaid SIM at the airport or a mall. Have your passport handy for registration.

Browse Globe’s traveler options ↗
02

Carry a little cash

Cards work well at hotels, malls, and many restaurants, but pesos are still useful for small shops, tips, and some transport. Use a bank ATM or official money changer, and keep smaller notes on hand.

03

Let the weather set the pace

January is comfortably warm and generally dry. Pack breathable layers, sunscreen, and one light piece for ambitious air-conditioning. English is widely spoken, so asking for help is easy.

Move like a local-ish

Getting around Manila

Our strongest recommendation

Download Grab before you land.

It’s the simplest way to book a car, see the price up front, and get from NAIA to your hotel. Follow the airport signs for the designated pickup area, confirm the plate number in the app, and build in more time than you think you need—Manila traffic is a true character.

Wedding-day note: If you are traveling between Makati, BGC, or the airport, leave early. A buffer is a gift to your future self.

Keep in your pocket

  • Use Grab or a hotel-arranged car.
  • Choose metered taxis only if you do take one.
  • Pin your destination; neighbourhood names can be similar.
  • Have a card saved in Grab, or a little cash for tolls/tips.

A wedding-weekend essential

Finding a barong

A barong looks its best when it feels like you, not a costume.

The quick version

Buy it roomy, wear it untucked.

A Barong Tagalog is traditionally worn over a light undershirt, untucked, with tailored trousers and dress shoes. For a versatile first barong, a natural or ivory-toned jusi style is a lovely, practical choice; reserve delicate piña for when you want the heirloom-level version.

1

Start at Kultura for ready-to-wear styles, approachable price points, and locations at SM Aura, SM Makati, and Mall of Asia.

2

Try it on in person. Check the shoulder seam and sleeve length; the shirt should hang easily over your trousers.

3

Give yourself time. A tailored or embroidered piece may need alterations, especially close to the wedding.
